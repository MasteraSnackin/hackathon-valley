PROPERTY DESK — EDITABLE JUDGEMENT AND HUMAN REVIEW PLAN
10 October 2026
Implementation authorised by the user. This build-only copy preserves the agreed generic design and removes all case-specific examples and all-corpus references.

PURPOSE

Build a maintenance case desk that receives tenant requests, keeps staff informed, and moves authorised work forward. Vercel Workflows preserves progress and manages waits. A separate, editable policy layer decides when the agent may act, request clarification, wait, ask for approval or stop a particular action.

The essential product promise: change routine conditions and escalation routes through settings, preview their effect, and publish them without editing prompts or redeploying the workflow. New combinations of supported conditions need no code. A genuinely new integration, action or evidence detector may still need development.

This plan groups the existing property-case audit into practical rules. Recommended responses and timing values are proposed design defaults; the dataset does not supply a complete operating policy.

1. KEEP FOUR RESPONSIBILITIES SEPARATE

Evidence: preserve original messages and documents, who supplied them, the property/job, when the event happened and when the system received it. Store extracted claims with links to their sources. Retain conflicting claims rather than overwriting one.

AI interpretation: identify the request, separate issues, extract candidate facts, identify possible conflicts and propose the next action. The model must distinguish a source assertion from a verified fact. It cannot change policy, grant itself authority or resolve missing facts by guessing.

Policy decisions: check the proposed action against explicit requirements and the selected policy version. Produce the allowed action, all blockers, the next permitted step, the responsible person and the evidence needed to resume.

Workflow execution: carry out permitted steps, wait for messages or human decisions, schedule bounded follow-ups, verify results and update the timeline. Recheck the policy before each consequential action.

Use one durable case coordinator, an application database for evidence/policies/reviews/action records, and attachment storage. The application database supports the staff dashboard and policy editor; Vercel's workflow history supports durable execution and technical inspection.

2. DECIDE ABOUT INDIVIDUAL ACTIONS

Evaluate such actions as asking for information, requesting a quote, confirming an appointment, instructing work, supplying access instructions, sending a reminder and closing an item separately.

For each action, every required check returns:
- PASS: the applicable requirement is supported by current evidence.
- FAIL: the requirement is explicitly not satisfied, such as access being refused.
- UNKNOWN: the required fact is absent or unreadable.
- CONFLICT: relevant sources make incompatible claims about the same matter, time and scope.

An apparent inconsistency starts as unresolved context, not a proven contradiction. A later explicit correction can supersede an earlier claim without erasing it. A later timestamp alone is insufficient.

The available outcomes are:
- ACT: all applicable requirements pass and no required approval remains outstanding.
- CLARIFY: ask a specific factual question through a verified, permitted route.
- WAIT: the question or decision is already pending; record the reply expected and deadline.
- HUMAN REVIEW: ask an authorised person for a specific judgement or scoped approval.
- STOP ACTION: the proposed action is refused, prohibited or outside the supported process. Another permitted action can be proposed.
- NO FURTHER ACTION: evidence supports resolution or an authorised handover; record the reason.

A contradiction is material only if resolving it could change whether this action is allowed or correct. Irrelevant uncertainty remains recorded without blocking unrelated work.

Evaluate all applicable rules. An ACT result from one rule cannot override another rule's blocker. Explicit prohibitions prevent execution; unresolved permissions, material evidence gaps and required approvals also prevent execution. Safe clarification can continue. Only the authorised owner can amend an authority record; switching off immediate escalation does not manufacture permission.

3. MAKE THE SETTINGS EDITOR THE MAIN CONTROL SURFACE

Give each rule a readable sentence: “When [condition] affects [action], [response], owned by [role].”

Show simple controls first:
- Applies to: organisation, landlord/property profile, work category and action.
- Condition: a supported fact or check; is missing, conflicts, is refused, exceeds a limit or is overdue. Allow labelled ALL / ANY condition groups.
- Response: clarify first, wait, human review immediately or stop the action.
- Owner: the role responsible for the decision, with a deputy.
- Resume requirement: the specific evidence or approval that releases this blocker.

Put required evidence, accepted sources, follow-up interval, reminder limit, deadline, expiry and notification channel under expandable options. Use dropdowns and numeric controls. Show a plain-language preview beside every rule.

Keep two types of setting visibly separate:
- Resolution preferences: how quickly to escalate, who receives it and how many follow-ups are permitted.
- Action requirements: identity, authority, consent and the evidence required for this action. Changes require an authorised policy editor and an explicit published version.

Example setting:
Condition: entry permission is unknown for the proposed access method.
Immediate human review: off.
Response: request permission from the verified authorised person, then wait.
If switched on: send a focused human review instead.
In both settings, entry remains blocked until its requirement is satisfied.

For scope, use organisation defaults plus an explicitly selected property/landlord profile. Show the resulting effective policy. Do not silently choose between incompatible profiles: hold the affected action for policy-owner review. Case-specific approvals are recorded decisions, not hidden changes to global settings.

4. INITIAL RULES GROUNDED IN THE PROPERTY CASES

The case numbers below refer to the audited public snapshot. They illustrate the condition; the proposed operational response is our design.

R1 — PROPERTY, PERSON OR ROLE IS UNCLEAR
Trigger: no verified mapping connects the proposed recipient, property, job or approver to the action, or relevant references disagree.
Default: try trusted stored mappings. If unresolved, ask the case manager; a clarification may go only through an already verified route. Hold identity-dependent disclosure, booking and result attribution.
Editable: trusted mapping sources, accepted aliases, role requirements and review owner.
Resume: verified mapping and authority for this action. Similar names or addresses alone are insufficient.

R2 — ACCESS OR KEY ARRANGEMENTS ARE NOT ESTABLISHED
Trigger: access permission is absent, disputed, withdrawn or does not cover the proposed visit/method; required key custody evidence is missing.
Default: ask for the missing permission or custody confirmation. Explicit refusal stops that access method; conflicting permission goes to review. A changed arrangement triggers another scope check.
Editable: immediate-review switch, accepted permission channels, access scope, reconfirmation requirements, key custodian and return deadline.
Resume: applicable recorded consent or other documented access authority, plus custody evidence required for the proposed handover. Availability at home does not establish permission to use management keys.

R3 — APPROVAL DOES NOT COVER THE WORK
Trigger: the proposed instruction exceeds the approved scope, substitutes a provider outside delegated authority, or treats a quote/inspection instruction as permission to repair.
Default: request a scoped decision from the authorised approver. Continue separately authorised information gathering and work items.
Editable: actions needing approval, delegated scope, approver roles, provider selection requirements and approval expiry.
Resume: approval covering the actual property, work, provider and applicable conditions. Existing approval covers only its stated scope.

R4 — DATES OR APPOINTMENTS CANNOT BE RECONCILED
Trigger: date and weekday disagree; proposed slots conflict; document/service dates would change the decision; a missed visit requires a new arrangement.
Default: seek a precise confirmation before issuing a dependent booking or reminder. Accept an explicit correction from an appropriate source as an update, then recheck access.
Editable: required confirming parties, time zone, date/window precision, rescheduling authority and escalation deadline.
Resume: an agreed active slot or corrected material date linked to the job. Cancel superseded reminders. Never silently repair an ambiguous date.

R5 — REQUIRED EVIDENCE IS MISSING OR UNREADABLE
Trigger: an action requires a fact, report, photograph or document that is unavailable, a placeholder, the wrong document or unsuccessfully parsed.
Default: request the specific material or an allowed substitute. Escalate if the limit is reached or a human must extract it. Do not block because an irrelevant attachment is absent.
Editable: evidence required per action, acceptable substitutes/formats, extraction retries and review owner.
Resume: usable case-linked evidence meeting the configured requirement. A policy-authorised exception must name the waived requirement and remaining uncertainty; it cannot assert that an unseen photograph was inspected.

R6 — THE AI'S INTERPRETATION DOES NOT MATCH THE REQUEST
Trigger: extracted category, location, symptom or object conflicts with the underlying message, or more than one interpretation would change the next action.
Default: correct a derived label automatically when the source is explicit and the configured policy allows it; otherwise ask a targeted question. Re-evaluate trade, urgency and authority after any correction.
Editable: allowed automatic corrections, required intake facts, categories requiring human review and acceptable source types.
Resume: a source-supported interpretation. Inferences remain labelled as inferences and cannot satisfy permission checks.

R7 — A MATERIAL SOURCE CONFLICT REMAINS
Trigger: two relevant claims about the same item, scope and time remain incompatible after checking identity, versions and explicit corrections.
Default: human review immediately for an unresolved material contradiction. Apparent inconsistencies or a missing fact use clarification first when a verified person can supply the answer.
Editable: clarify-first versus immediate-review route for eligible conflict categories, accepted sources by fact type, adjudicating role and required supporting evidence.
Resume: an evidenced correction, verified scope distinction or appropriately authorised decision addressing the particular conflict. Record why one claim was superseded; do not erase the other source.

R8 — COMPLETION OR CLOSURE IS NOT SUPPORTED
Trigger: required completion evidence is missing, some work remains open, a promised follow-up remains, or a completion report conflicts with relevant evidence.
Default: request missing confirmation; send a material conflict or proposed cancellation of a commitment to review. Evaluate each work item separately.
Editable: accepted completion evidence by work type, who may confirm, tenant confirmation requirement, partial closure and administrative archive policy.
Resume: every item being marked resolved meets its evidence rule, and relevant commitments are completed or explicitly cancelled by an authorised person. Administrative archive, handover and verified resolution remain separate statuses.

R9 — A REPLY OR HUMAN DECISION IS OVERDUE
Trigger: an open question has no relevant response by its configured deadline.
Default: send only the allowed reminders, then route to the named owner. While a valid request is pending and not due, wait. Silence never creates consent, approval or completion evidence.
Editable: working hours, reminder interval/count, deadline, deputy and human review escalation.
Resume: a relevant answer or decision. A timer permits reassessment or a permitted reminder only.

R10 — AUTOMATION NO LONGER MATCHES CURRENT STATE
Trigger: a queued chase is superseded, a completed task reappears, or a new message belongs to a different issue.
Default: refresh case state before sending. Cancel demonstrably stale reminders, separate new issues, and request technical review if state cannot be reconciled. Reopen a resolved item only on relevant evidence or an authorised instruction.
Editable: authoritative state source, reopening rules, freshness requirements and technical owner.
Resume: current state is reconciled, the proposed action is still relevant and its previous execution status is known.

R11 — FINANCIAL COMMITMENT NEEDS CLARIFICATION OR APPROVAL
Trigger: the full commitment is unknown, quote/invoice/approval disagree, or cumulative same-job commitment exceeds the review threshold or existing authority.
Default: clarify unknown amounts and tax/scope differences; request scoped approval where required. Keep payment state separate from repair state.
Editable: review threshold, accepted quote fields, variance-review tolerance, approval owner and budget aggregation. A variance tolerance cannot enlarge actual authority.
Resume: a comparable total and scope are established, all authority checks pass and required review is complete. Unknown is not zero. For an illustrative £100 inclusive review limit, £100 is within it and £100.01 triggers review.

5. HANDLE NEW CASES WITHOUT INVENTING FACTS

Add two general fallback policies alongside the observed rule families:

Unsupported or novel situation: if no supported action/requirement mapping applies, hold the consequential action, gather permitted information and route it to the case manager. The AI may propose a new draft rule for later review; it cannot publish it itself.

Potential urgent hazard: apply an organisation-approved urgency and response policy. If the report may be urgent and no applicable procedure exists, route immediately to the designated human and hold unsupported instructions. Missing safety answers alone do not prove an emergency. The audited corpus does not supply a complete emergency policy.

6. BOUND CLARIFICATION AND HUMAN REVIEW

Proposed non-urgent demonstration defaults: one initial clarification request, at most one reminder after one working day, and human escalation after two working days without a relevant answer. Use Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00 Europe/London for this sample profile. These values are editable demonstration choices, not promised service levels; urgent categories bypass this schedule.

For this profile, one working day means eight hours within that schedule, counted from the initial request; escalation follows sixteen such hours. Before acting on a timer, process any durably received reply and confirm the question is still unanswered. A real operating profile also needs its holiday calendar.

Count reminders per unresolved question, not per incoming message. A reply that does not answer the question does not reset the original deadline. A changed question gets a new tracked request; the overall case deadline still applies. After human escalation, route an overdue review to the named deputy, keep affected actions held and keep the item visible. Never fall back to automatic approval.

Every human review card contains:
- The proposed action and the exact question to decide.
- The affected property and work item.
- Missing or conflicting evidence, with source links.
- The rule and policy version that caused the hold.
- Other blockers that this decision will not resolve.
- What will happen next if the requirement is satisfied.

Provide buttons appropriate to the question: supply/correct a fact, request more evidence, approve the specified action within authority, reject/reassign the action, or defer with a deadline. Require a reason/source for a correction and scope/expiry for an approval where applicable.

An employee resolving identity, a person giving access consent and a manager approving work are different roles. Validate the responder's role in the application. A generic approval cannot substitute for another person's required consent.

7. VERCEL WORKFLOW BEHAVIOUR

Intake validates and durably stores an incoming event before signalling its case coordinator. Duplicate messages receive the same event identity. The coordinator processes new events, updates evidence, evaluates proposed actions, executes permitted steps and waits for the next message, review response or timer.

Represent human reviews as open case records while the coordinator continues receiving other events. Do not freeze all case intake behind one approval. Use one ordered consumer for case events; a durable incoming-event record lets the system recover a signal that arrives before the waiting hook is ready.

Vercel hooks support pause/resume with external data; durable timers support deadlines. The application must authenticate and authorise the reviewer before resuming a workflow. A hook token identifies a wait and does not by itself authorise an approval. Use a single event-consumption path so competing waits cannot swallow each other's responses.

After any response or timer: load current case facts, check the review/action revision, check current authority and the applicable policy, and evaluate every blocker again. Duplicate, expired, superseded or wrong-role decisions must not execute an action. Record late replies as evidence even when the original approval request is no longer valid.

For outbound actions, record intent and a stable business-action identity before execution. Use external duplicate-prevention support where available and read back the result. A lost response does not prove failure: reconcile an uncertain booking/send before retrying. Stop and surface technical review if it cannot be reconciled. Workflow retries alone do not guarantee that an external action happens exactly once.

The staff dashboard reads the case record and shows stage, evidence, last verified action, next step, blockers and owner. Technical workflow completion is not proof that physical repair work is complete.

8. CHANGE POLICIES WITHOUT SURPRISES

Use Draft → Test on saved cases → Compare effects → Publish. Store immutable policy versions with author, reason and effective time. Preserve the version and evidence snapshot used for every decision.

Publishing applies the selected policy to new assessments. For existing pending decisions, show which cases are affected and require explicit reassessment before a changed rule releases an action. A relaxed threshold never silently turns an old pending request into an executable instruction. Revoked consent or authority is checked before the next action regardless of a historical approval.

Before execution, compare the recorded decision's case/policy revision with the currently applicable revision. If changed, reevaluate or leave it held for the required reassessment. Completed actions retain their original history; uncertain actions are reconciled before replacing them.

Keep policy versions distinct from workflow code versions. Vercel normally pins an existing workflow run to the deployment that started it. Load an explicit policy snapshot at each fresh assessment and persist the result; do not rely on rereading a previously completed step to refresh settings. A policy using a condition that an older running workflow cannot understand must stay held until that workflow is deliberately upgraded or restarted safely.

Rollback publishes another policy version; it does not undo completed real-world actions.

9. DELIVERY ORDER AND ACCEPTANCE CHECKS

First: define the action list, role map and required evidence from the agreed plan and BUILD cases only. Create staged BUILD snapshots that expose only the history available at the decision point.

Second: build the policy evaluator and simple settings form. Demonstrate that changing clarification versus immediate review changes the route while missing permission still blocks the affected action.

Third: connect case intake, event storage, Vercel waits/timers and the human review inbox. Demonstrate that new evidence can arrive while a review is pending and that a valid decision resumes the case after rechecking.

Fourth: add permitted action tools and the visible timeline. Start external communications/contractor actions as clearly labelled simulations; only claim real execution once the relevant integration and result are verified.

Fifth: test on BUILD cases and new build-derived scenarios only. Freeze the implementation, policies and rubric for a separate evaluator. Do not inspect or execute the reserved evaluation cases. The generic policy design already reflects a prior all-corpus audit, so the later evaluation is reserved rather than previously unseen.

Acceptance scenarios:
- Missing access consent: clarification with the toggle off; human review with it on; entry blocked in both.
- Two blockers: resolving one leaves the other active.
- Explicit date correction: superseded appointment reminders stop without unnecessary review.
- Missing photograph: no invented visual observation.
- Partial completion: only evidenced items can close.
- Unanswered request: bounded reminders, then human review; no automatic consent or completion.
- Policy changes during a wait: visible reassessment before any release.
- Duplicate/late approval or incoming message: no duplicate action.
- Lost external acknowledgement: verify outcome before retrying.
- Unsupported new case: useful information gathering plus a visible human handover.

Measure correct next actions, unsupported actions, unnecessary escalations, valid resumption, duplicate actions and unresolved work incorrectly closed. Human-labelled acceptable outcomes can include more than one reasonable next step. Do not use eventual historical closure as an automatic ground-truth answer.
